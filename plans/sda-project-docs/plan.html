Why both 02-requirements.md and 03-prd.md? They are not the same document and it is easy to confuse them.

Requirements is the engineering list: numbered FR-01…, NFR-01…, each testable, each traced to a use case. It is the graded Requirements Model.
PRD is the product view: who the users are, what success looks like, what is in and out of scope, what could go wrong. It is what you would hand a teammate to explain why the system exists.

The main work

Six use cases in, seven out

The brief lists six names. Names are not specifications — none of them say what happens when something fails. Each becomes a full spec: ID, actor, preconditions, trigger, main success scenario, extensions, postconditions.

IDUse caseWhat changes
UC-01 Join Waiting Room Harden Adds the rules the brief skips: one active token per account per event (stops queue-jumping with ten tabs), reconnect keeps the same position, and what happens when the sale has already sold out.
UC-02 Select & Hold Tickets Harden The 10-minute expiry becomes an explicit extension flow that returns seats to the pool. Adds: asked for more than remain, over the per-order limit.
UC-03 Checkout & Pay Harden Three failure paths written out — card declined, hold expired mid-payment, and the same order submitted twice.
UC-04 View Digital Ticket Define "Verifiable" gets a meaning: a signed, single-use QR payload. Scanning at the gate is declared out of scope, on purpose, in writing.
UC-05 Create Event with Tiers Split The brief writes this as one name with a slash: "Create / Manage Event & Tiers". Creating and editing have different preconditions, so they are two use cases. Seven total still fits the brief's 5–7 range.
UC-06 Manage Event & Tiers Split
UC-07 Monitor Live Queue & Sales Define Names the actual numbers on the screen: queue length, admission rate, and held / sold / remaining per tier.

Actors stay at two: Customer and Organizer. Keeping one human actor per side means the use-case diagram stays readable — no system actors, no API actors.

Analysis

Three real gaps in the brief

These are not style problems. Each one is something a grader can ask about and the brief has no answer for.

Gap in the brief

Nobody owns the expiring hold

The 10-minute TTL is the single mechanism that stops overselling, and no use case is responsible for it. Seats are locked by UC-02 and nothing in the brief ever unlocks them.

Fix — expiry becomes a named extension flow inside UC-02, not a new use case. It is triggered by time, not by a person, so giving it its own bubble would put a clock in the actor column of the diagram.

Gap in the brief

Payment and the hold can race each other

What if the hold expires while the payment is in flight? The customer is charged and has no seat, or keeps a seat somebody else was just sold. The brief does not mention it.

Fix — UC-03 gets an explicit extension: if the hold is gone when the payment result arrives, the payment is voided and no ticket is issued. Written down, so the implementation has no choice to make.

Gap in the brief

No non-functional requirements at all

The brief lists only things the system does. This is an architecture course — Lecture 3.1 is explicit that architecture characteristics are what justify a design, and Lecture 5.1 is about measuring them. A design section with no characteristics has nothing to defend.

Fix — three driving characteristics, each with a number attached.

The three characteristics this system is designed for

CharacteristicPlain meaningHow it gets measured
ElasticityStay responsive during a sudden spike, not a slow climb. Lecture 3.1 separates this from scalability and the separation matters here — a ticket sale is all spike.Concurrent joiners go from ~100 to 50,000 inside one minute and queue positions still issue.
Data integrityTickets sold never exceed tickets that exist. No exceptions, no reconciliation afterwards.200 buyers race for the last 10 seats; sold count is exactly 10, every time.
PerformanceThe queue screen tells the truth quickly enough to be believed.Position refresh within 5 s; a hold is placed or refused within 300 ms.

Deliberately not prioritised, to avoid the overengineering Lecture 3.1 warns about: internationalisation, offline use, and extensibility beyond the two plug-in seams below.

Design

Two styles, mapped to the slides

The brief names both styles but not the parts. 06-architecture.md fills them in, using the exact vocabulary from Lecture 8 so the mapping is checkable.

Slide 8.1 lists four risks. The document answers each: filters stay single-purpose, pipes stay one-way, a rejected order leaves through its own consumer rather than a pipe pointing backwards, and the contract between filters is one small record.

Commitments

Decisions that are expensive to undo

These get written into the documents as stated decisions, not options. Changing them later means rewriting the domain model and the flows.

01

The hold is the unit of truth, not the payment

Inventory goes down the moment a seat is held, not when money arrives. This is what makes overselling structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely.

Cost — expired holds must reliably return stock, or seats leak out of the sale forever.

02

The 10-minute clock is the server's

The TTL is stamped when the hold is created and judged server-side. The countdown the customer sees is display only.

Cost — the customer's timer and the server's can disagree by a second or two near zero.

03

The queue token carries identity through the whole purchase

One token means one checkout attempt. It ties the queue, the hold and the order together, which is what lets the system spot a duplicate submit.

Cost — a customer who loses their token starts over at the back of the queue.

04

PaymentProvider and PricingRule are the only plug-in seams

Every payment method and every promotion depends on these two interface shapes, so they are fixed now, in writing, before anything is built against them.

Cost — anything that is neither a payment method nor a pricing rule has to change the core.

05

AGENTS.md is the one source of rules

All project rules live in AGENTS.md. CLAUDE.md is a single pointer line. Codex reads AGENTS.md natively; Command Code reads one of the two. One file to edit, three tools kept in step.

Cost — if a tool ever needs different rules, the split has to be reintroduced.

Decided

Four things decided

All four were answered with the default shown. Kept here as the record of the decision.

1 · Seventh use case — split, or add gate validation?

Splitting "Create / Manage" gives seven. The alternative is keeping it merged and spending the seventh slot on Validate Ticket at Gate, which would give "verifiable ticket" a real home — but it adds a third actor (gate staff) to the diagram.

DefaultSplit into UC-05 and UC-06. Keeps two actors, keeps the diagram clean.

2 · Should this become a git repo?

It is a plain folder right now. Version history would help, and the group may want a shared remote later.

Defaultgit init locally, first commit, no remote until you ask.

3 · Real payment integration, or simulated?

Stripe and PromptPay are named in the brief. Actually integrating either is a large job on its own.

DefaultSimulated plug-ins behind the real PaymentProvider contract. The architecture is demonstrated; no API keys needed.

4 · Diagrams now, or after you approve the text?

The use-case diagram, SSD and activity diagram are Member 3's deliverables. They depend on the use-case text being settled.

DefaultText first. Once the seven use cases are agreed, the diagrams get built from them — nothing to redraw.
